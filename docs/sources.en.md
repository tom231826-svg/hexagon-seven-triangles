# Earlier work on the hexagon problem

[한국어](sources.md) · [English overview](../README.en.md)

Dmytro Karabash and Alexander Soifer posed the hexagon problem in their 2005 paper *On Covering of Trigons*. Soifer later restated their theorem that eight unit equilateral triangles suffice to cover a slightly enlarged regular unit hexagon. In a 2014 lecture, he identified the impossibility of covering it with seven as their 2005 conjecture. [2005 journal contents](https://geombina.uccs.edu/past-issues/volume-xv) · [Soifer's 2010 restatement, Theorem 3, printed p. 35](https://www.wfnmc.org/Journal%202010%201.pdf#page=39) · [2014 lecture, p. 18](https://onemperu.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/alexandersoifer.pdf#page=18)

**Jineon Baek and Jeewon Kim proved the hexagon theorem before this project did.** Their repository contains a July 2026 statement and proof of the main theorem and a 69-page manuscript dated September 24. Baek's separate repository records a Lean formalization on September 27. This project completed its internal review of its geometric proof on November 11 and subsequently formalized the result in Lean. The dates refer to the documents and their commit histories. The dates on which the authors first made their repositories public, and the journal review status of their manuscript, remain unconfirmed. [July main-theorem commit](https://github.com/dylan0301/hexagon-cover-database/commit/ec5efdc538a96fcfb84910fee1875c99e7e62a61) · [September joint manuscript](https://github.com/dylan0301/hexagon-cover-database/blob/47a136fcdb3b31d46d0bb1a54da81c2cf73b8222/arrange/paper_draft/main.pdf) · [Baek's Lean formalization](https://github.com/jcpaik/hexagon-cover-formalization/tree/545a609e0225e664dc830310641f2ec0170ee181)

## Does the earlier proof imply the 105-point result?

Using a finite collection of points and considering which triangle covers each point were already part of the earlier work. The question here is more specific. For the **same 39 points with prescribed triangles and 66 freely assignable points**, every assignment fails if the distance from the origin to the triangle $T_5$ is at least $81\sqrt{3}/200$. Removing that single condition permits an explicit placement of all seven triangles. [Exact input, placement and certificates](../finite/README.md)

In the no-gap case, Baek and Kim choose nine points whose locations depend on the boundary parameters, then prove that the same central triangle would have to contain all nine. In the 105-point problem, each of the 66 free points may go to any of the seven triangles. We have not established that covering these fixed points forces the boundary and interior conditions needed for the earlier argument. [Baek and Kim's Appendix E](https://github.com/dylan0301/hexagon-cover-database/blob/47a136fcdb3b31d46d0bb1a54da81c2cf73b8222/arrange/paper_draft/E_zero_gap_nine_point_optimization.tex) · [Comparison of the two proofs](proof-comparison.en.md)

As of November 18, 2026, our search of public manuscripts, the authors' repositories and build archives, and scholarly indexes had found no earlier result establishing both outcomes for this point set and clearance condition. Two texts remain unavailable to us in full.

- A hybrid manuscript dated September 26 cites a 105-page manuscript dated September 25, *Seven open triangles cannot cover a closed hexagon*, as P2. Its reference gives neither an author nor a public link. The hybrid manuscript's version of the no-gap argument obtains a point common to three triangles by assuming coverage of an entire triangular sector. Whether another appendix of P2 proves a finite-point result remains open. [Hybrid manuscript, Lemma 5.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jcpaik/hexagon-cover-formalization/545a609e0225e664dc830310641f2ec0170ee181/hexagon_hybrid_proof.pdf#page=12) · [P2 bibliography entry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jcpaik/hexagon-cover-formalization/545a609e0225e664dc830310641f2ec0170ee181/hexagon_hybrid_proof.pdf#page=16)
- Karabash and Soifer's original paper appeared in *Geombinatorics* **15**(1), pp. 13–17. We read the two later accounts linked above. Whether the original five pages contained a separate finite-point lemma remains unverified.
